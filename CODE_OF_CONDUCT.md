# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want Poop Linux to be a project where people can contribute without fear of harassment, whatever their background, identity, experience or role. We pledge to make participation a harassment-free experience for everyone.

## Our standards

Behaviour that helps the project:

- Being respectful of differing viewpoints and experience levels.
- Giving and accepting constructive criticism on code and designs. Criticise the work, not the person.
- Taking responsibility for mistakes, apologising and learning from them.
- Focusing on what is best for the project and its users.
- Being direct and clear, and assuming good faith.

Behaviour that is not acceptable:

- Harassment, insults, personal attacks, or trolling, in any channel.
- Sexualised content or unwanted sexual attention.
- Publishing others' private information (doxxing) without consent.
- Threats, intimidation or stalking.
- Repeatedly ignoring review feedback or project rules in a way that wastes maintainers' time.
- Any other conduct that a reasonable person would consider inappropriate in a professional setting.

The project's name is playful. Playfulness is welcome in discussion and in documentation, but it must not be used to demean people, and it stays out of security advisories, error messages and package descriptions.

## Scope

This code applies in all project spaces: issues, pull requests, reviews, chat channels, events, and any public representation of the project. It also applies when you represent the project in public.

## Reporting

Report conduct concerns privately to a maintainer listed in [`MAINTAINERS.md`](MAINTAINERS.md). If a maintainer is involved, contact another maintainer. Include what happened, where, and any links or screenshots. Do not post the details publicly.

Maintainers will treat reports confidentially, acknowledge them within a week, and decide on a response. Reporters may ask for a review of a decision.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may take the following actions, in proportion to the violation:

1. **Correction:** a private or public note explaining what was wrong, and what is expected.
2. **Warning:** a formal notice, with a clear description of the consequence of further violations.
3. **Temporary restriction:** removal from discussion or contribution for a stated period.
4. **Permanent ban:** removal from project spaces, for serious or repeated violations.

Maintainers who are party to a report do not decide on it.
